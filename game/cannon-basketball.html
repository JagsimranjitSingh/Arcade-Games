Game Overview

Cannon Basketball is a physics-based puzzle shooter where you fire basketballs from a rotating cannon, bouncing them off walls and past obstacles to hit the target hoop. Each level presents a unique geometric arrangement of walls, barriers, and moving elements, and you must calculate the perfect angle and power to thread the ball through the course. It's part geometry, part physics intuition, and entirely addictive.

The cannon's angle is controlled by tapping/clicking and dragging — a trajectory preview line shows the ball's initial path, but only the first bounce. Beyond that first ricochet, you must mentally predict the ball's path through the remaining geometry. This limited preview system ensures that solving each puzzle requires genuine spatial reasoning rather than simple line tracing.

Level design progresses through five themed chapters. Chapter 1 (Training Court) teaches basic angle and power mechanics with simple wall bounces. Chapter 2 (Bounce House) introduces convex and concave surfaces that redirect the ball in unexpected ways. Chapter 3 (Moving Parts) adds shifting walls, rotating barriers, and platforms that slide along tracks. Chapter 4 (Trick Shots) demands multi-bounce precision through tight corridors and ricochet chains. Chapter 5 (Championship) combines every mechanic with minimal error tolerance — each shot must be perfect.

Star ratings on each level are based on shot count: completing a level in one shot earns 3 stars, two shots earns 2 stars, and three or more earns 1 star. This three-tier rating system adds significant replay value, as levels that seemed challenging to complete at all become new puzzles when you try to solve them in a single masterful shot. The game also features a level editor where you can create and share custom puzzles, extending the content indefinitely. Over 120 hand-crafted levels and a thriving custom level community ensure that Cannon Basketball never runs short of fresh challenges.

Gameplay Strategy & Pro Tips

Reading the Trajectory Preview

The preview line shows your ball's path up to and including the first bounce. Use this to aim precisely at the wall surface you want to ricochet from. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection — visualize the second bounce by mentally "mirroring" the preview line across the wall surface. With practice, you can predict 2-3 bounces mentally from just the initial preview.

Power Control

Drag distance controls shot power. Short drags produce soft, lobbing arcs ideal for precise short-range placements. Long drags create powerful shots that travel fast and far, bouncing with more energy. In tight multi-bounce corridors, reduce power to keep bounces controllable. In long-distance courses, full power preserves energy through multiple wall contacts.

Moving Element Timing

In Chapter 3+, moving barriers and platforms follow predictable cycles. Watch at least one full cycle before shooting. Identify the moment when your desired path is clear, then work backward from that timing — calculate how long the ball takes to reach the moving element, and fire that many seconds before the window opens. Don't rush; patience with timing yields first-shot solutions.

3-Star Single Shot Solutions

Most levels have a "designer intended" single-shot solution. Look for the path that uses the fewest bounces — it's almost always the optimal route. Key indicators include surfaces angled toward the hoop, convex walls positioned as "redirectors," and gaps in barriers that align at specific angles. The elegant solution often uses the geometry that seems most prominent in the level layout.
