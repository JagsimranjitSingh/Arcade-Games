Game Overview

Chroma Sort is a mesmerizing color-sorting puzzle game where you pour vibrant neon liquids between translucent test tubes until each tube contains only a single, pure color. Starting with tubes filled with randomized layers of different colored liquids, you must carefully plan your pour sequence — each tube can only hold four layers, and you can only pour a color onto the same color or into an empty tube.

The game's physics-inspired pouring animation brings a tactile quality to every move. Liquids flow smoothly between tubes with realistic viscosity effects, and completing a tube triggers a satisfying chromatic burst animation. While the mechanics are simple enough for anyone to grasp instantly, the strategic depth emerges in puzzles with 8 or more colors, where efficient solving requires thinking several moves ahead and managing temporary "buffer" tubes to facilitate complex sorting chains.

Chroma Sort features 300 levels divided into three acts. Act I (Prism) introduces 4-color puzzles with generous empty tube allocation. Act II (Spectrum) expands to 8 colors with fewer buffers, demanding more precise planning. Act III (Ultraviolet) challenges you with 12 colors, minimal buffers, and locked tubes that cannot receive pours until specific conditions are met. Each act also introduces visual modifier tubes — heated tubes that change the color of any liquid poured in, and frozen tubes that lock their current contents until thawed by completing an adjacent tube.

Beyond the structured level progression, a free play mode generates infinite random puzzles at your chosen color count, ensuring the game never runs out of content. The minimalist neon-dark UI keeps focus squarely on the colorful liquids, and the ambient soundscape creates a calm atmosphere perfect for extended puzzle sessions. Chroma Sort has become one of the most popular casual puzzle experiences on the platform, appealing to both color-matching enthusiasts and strategic thinkers alike.

Gameplay Strategy & Pro Tips

Buffer Tube Management

Empty tubes are your most valuable resource. Never fill a buffer tube with mixed colors — it defeats the purpose. Use buffers exclusively for temporarily storing a single color while you sort other tubes. In harder levels with limited buffers, plan your entire solving sequence before making the first pour.

The Bottom-Layer Principle

Always think about bottom layers first. A tube's bottom color determines what that tube will eventually become when fully sorted. Before pouring colors on top of anything, ask: "Is this bottom color aligned with my sorting plan?" Moving bottom layers requires emptying the entire tube above them, so establishing correct bottom-layer placement early saves enormous backtracking.

Multi-Pour Chain Planning

Expert-level puzzles require planning 5-10 pours ahead. Look for "chain opportunities" where completing one pour creates the conditions for the next. For example: pour blue from tube A to tube C, which frees the top of tube A to receive red from tube B, which then exposes green in tube B for its final destination. Thinking in chains rather than individual pours is the difference between efficient solving and getting stuck.

Dealing With Locked Tubes

In Act III, locked tubes add a puzzle-within-a-puzzle element. Identify what condition unlocks each locked tube (usually completing an adjacent tube), and work backward from those conditions. Sometimes you need to solve a specific non-locked tube first purely to unlock a critical locked tube — recognizing these dependencies early prevents dead-end solving paths.

Frequently Asked Questions About Chroma Sort

How does pouring work in Chroma Sort?

Tap a source tube, then tap a destination tube to pour. Liquid can only be poured onto the same color or into an empty tube, and each tube holds a maximum of four layers. When you pour, all consecutive layers of the same color transfer at once — if the top three layers of a tube are all blue, all three pour together.

What are heated and frozen tubes?

Heated tubes (Act III) change the color of any liquid poured into them, adding a transformation puzzle element. Frozen tubes lock their current contents and refuse incoming pours until they are thawed — which happens when you complete (fill with a single color) an adjacent tube. These special tube types create additional strategic constraints.

Is there a way to play endlessly beyond the 300 levels?

Yes — Chroma Sort features a free play mode that generates infinite random puzzles. You can choose the number of colors (4-12) and buffer tubes to customize difficulty. Free play puzzles are guaranteed solvable and provide endless content after completing the structured level progression.

What strategies help with 8+ color puzzles?

For complex puzzles, the key strategies are: (1) never contaminate buffer tubes with mixed colors, (2) plan bottom-layer placement before making any pours, (3) think in multi-pour chains rather than single moves, and (4) identify and prioritize 'dependency tubes' whose completion unlocks other solving paths. Patience and planning trump speed in high-color-count puzzles.
